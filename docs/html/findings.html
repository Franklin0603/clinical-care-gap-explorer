Twenty-one patients three mistakes would have hidden

Seven things that came out of building a care-gap pipeline, and the numbers behind each one.

Synthea synthetic data · Massachusetts · 1,000 patients · as of 2026-08-23
No real patient information. Every figure below is read from the pipeline output.

25of 116 living diabetic patients have had no A1c test in twelve months — 21.6%

21 of those 25 have never had one at all. They carry a diabetic complication but no diabetes diagnosis code. They are on no diabetes medication. And most were seen by a clinician in the weeks before the cut-off — one of them the day before.

They are not lost to the system. They are in the building, and nobody has ordered the one test that says how bad it is.

01

One diagnosis code misses 45% of the cohort

The obvious definition is "patients with the type 2 diabetes code, 44054006". It yields 88 patients.

Another 73 carry a complication of diabetes — diabetic kidney disease, retinopathy, neuropathy — with no underlying diagnosis code on file. Their record says, in effect, complication of a disease nobody wrote down.

Anchor on the single obvious code and 45% of the cohort disappears, specifically the sicker half. This is not an artefact of synthetic data. It is why published quality measures are defined by value sets — lists of dozens of codes — rather than by one code.

02

An inner join deletes the highest-risk patients, silently

The care-gap table is built by joining the cohort to each patient's most recent A1c. Written with an inner join it has 95 rows and zero never-tested patients. Written with a left join, 116 rows and 21.

The 21 patients it drops are 84% of the open gaps — the people who most need a call. A patient with no result has no row to join to, so an inner join removes exactly the population the report exists to find.

The pipeline now asserts on every run that never-tested patients are present and flagged. A test that fails when the bug returns is worth more than knowing about the bug.

03

The never-tested are the complication-only patients

Findings 1 and 2 are the same finding. Of the 21 patients who have never been tested:

  • 21 / 21carry no type 2 diabetes code
  • 21 / 21carry disorder of kidney due to diabetes mellitus
  • 0 / 21are on insulin — against 45 of the 95 tested patients
  • 0 / 21are on any diabetes medication at all

Most had been seen recently. The highest-priority patient on the worklist had an encounter the day before the cut-off.

The pattern: patients who are treated get monitored. Insulin comes with a pharmacist, a refill cycle and forced contact. A patient with a diabetic complication on the problem list and no diabetes prescription has nobody prompting the test.

Synthea's care model produces this. Real health systems do too.

04

Including the deceased inflated the gap nearly threefold

The first profiling figure was 69 of 161 — 43%. It counted 45 patients who had died before the cut-off, and the deceased carry most of the stale results because nobody orders tests for them. The report was partly measuring mortality.

A care-gap list is a call list. Excluding them gives 25 of 116. The number got smaller and less impressive, and it is the right one.

05

The specification's own plausibility range was wrong

The data quality spec proposed rejecting any A1c outside 3.0–20.0%. The units were right. The floor was not.

A check that fires on clean input makes the catch rate meaningless, and the catch rate is the number this project exists to report. Floor revised to 2.0.

The lesson is not that one threshold was wrong. It is that a threshold written from domain knowledge and never checked against the data is a guess wearing a lab coat.

06

"Same seed" did not mean same data

Regenerating with the original command four weeks later produced 1,151 patients instead of 1,142, with every table 4–6% larger.

The seed pins the patient generator. It does not pin the clinician seed, the reference date, or the simulation's end date — all three default to the wall clock, so running it a month later simulates a month more history. Each was found by pinning one and seeing what still moved.

Two lessons. The seed controls the data, not the calendar — any generator with a notion of "now" needs "now" pinned too. And reproducible means same content, not same bytes: the export is multithreaded, so row order varies between runs and comparing checksums reported a difference that did not exist.

07

A third of observations are words, not numbers

315,450 of 870,510 observations carry text results — smoking status, survey answers. Casting that column to a number would null every one of them while the row sat in the validated layer looking clean.

A silent drop in disguise, and the kind that survives review because nothing errors and the row count does not change. The validated layer keeps the original text for every row; the numeric column is populated only where the source was numeric, and the null counts reconcile.

How the checks were scored

249 rows damaged on purpose, in six realistic ways, each one logged. Without that log a catch rate is a claim — "I found twelve problems" says nothing about whether that was twelve of twelve or twelve of four hundred.

Six defect types249249

Each was caught by the check meant for it — a defect found by a different check is a coincidence, not a working check, and the score counts it as a miss. The 20 impossible A1c values were corrected rather than discarded, with the original kept and the row flagged, so a reviewer who disagrees can exclude them in one clause. The six duplicate registrations went to a human; nothing was merged, because a wrong merge combines two people's medication lists.

On every table, bronze = silver + quarantine balances, and the pipeline stops if it does not.

What the number cannot see

Stated here rather than left for a reader to discover.

Whether the test was ordered
There is no orders table. "We ordered it and the patient never went" and "we never ordered it" are the same row here. On real data they are different failures needing different interventions.
Results held somewhere else
Invisible. The measure is really "no result we can see", which is what every care-gap report measures.
Whether anyone can reach them
The source data has address and postcode but no phone and no email. The date they were last seen is the honest proxy.
Who is lost to follow-up
Zero of 116 — these synthetic patients never disappear. The column was dropped rather than shipped always-false.
Hospice and the other measure exclusions
Not implemented. This models the idea of a published quality measure, not the certified measure, and says so on every page.

Reproduce it

make generate   # Synthea, then the whole pipeline (~4 min, needs Java 17)
make run        # rebuild from existing data (~13 s)
make test       # 121 tests

Every figure on this page comes from the pipeline's own output files after a run. A clean clone, following the instructions exactly, reproduces all of them in 180 seconds — which is itself finding 6 being tested rather than asserted.
